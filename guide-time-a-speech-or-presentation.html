How to Time a Speech or Presentation

Estimate length from your word count, rehearse with a stopwatch and use a visible countdown on the day.

Running over your time slot is one of the most common speaking mistakes, and it tends to cost you the ending, which is the part the audience remembers. The fix is simple: measure your talk before you give it. A stopwatch for rehearsal and a countdown for the day do most of the work.

Estimate your length from the word count

Most people speak at roughly 120 to 160 words per minute in a presentation, with slower delivery for complex material and a faster pace for casual talks. Use about 130 words per minute for a first estimate.

Speaking timeApproximate words (at 130 wpm)
3 minutes390
5 minutes650
10 minutes1,300
20 minutes2,600

If you use slides, allow time for pauses, questions and transitions, and plan for less text than the table suggests.

Rehearse with the stopwatch

  1. Open the stopwatch. On a computer, Space starts and stops it and L records a lap.
  2. Start the stopwatch as you begin speaking, and say your talk aloud at the pace you intend to use.
  3. Press Lap at each section break: the opening, each main point and the conclusion.
  4. Stop at the end and read the list. Compare each section with your plan.

Rehearse aloud, not silently. Reading in your head is much faster than speaking, and will make your talk look shorter than it is.

Cut or adjust to fit

  • Too long: remove the weakest point rather than speeding up. Speaking faster makes you harder to follow.
  • Too short: add an example or a short story to your strongest point, not extra material for its own sake.
  • Uneven sections: if the opening takes a third of your time, shorten it. Audiences lose interest early.

Aim to finish a minute or so under your limit. Nerves, questions and slide changes nearly always add time on the day.

Use a countdown on the day

On the day, set the countdown timer to your speaking time and place it where you can see it, such as on a laptop at the front or a phone on the lectern. Use full-screen mode if you want large digits. If you have a helper, they can hold up a card at the 5-minute and 1-minute marks.

Check the timer at your planned section breaks, not constantly. If you are behind, skip an example rather than rushing the ending.

Tips for staying on schedule

  • Write the target time for each section in your notes, such as "Point 2: 4:00 to 7:00".
  • Practise the opening and closing most. They are the parts you must not lose to a time overrun.
  • Time your rehearsals several times. The first run is usually the longest.
  • For a group presentation, give each speaker a time limit and rehearse the hand-offs.
  • If a speaking competition or debate uses fixed times per speaker, use the chess clock to give each side equal time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words is a 5-minute speech?

At about 130 words per minute, a 5-minute speech is roughly 650 words. Slower delivery means fewer words.

What is a good speaking pace?

Most presentations run at about 120 to 160 words per minute. Slow down for complex or important points.

Should I rehearse out loud?

Yes. Reading silently is much faster than speaking and will make you underestimate the length of your talk.

Can I show the timer in full screen?

Yes. The tools have a full-screen mode that shows large digits, which is useful when you need to see the time from a distance.
