Pomodoro Timer

Work through focus sessions and breaks automatically: 25 minutes of focus, a 5-minute break and a longer break after every fourth session, all adjustable. A chime marks each change and the tab title shows the time left.

What Is a Pomodoro Timer?

A Pomodoro timer runs the cycle behind the Pomodoro Technique, a time-management method created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s and named after a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. You work in short, focused blocks (traditionally 25 minutes) separated by short breaks, with a longer break after every few blocks. The timer above keeps track of the phase and the session count for you, so you can stay on the task instead of watching a clock.

How This Pomodoro Timer Works

  • Focus: 25 minutes by default.
  • Short break: 5 minutes after each focus session.
  • Long break: 15 minutes after every fourth focus session.
  • Automatic switching: when a phase ends, a chime plays and the next phase starts right away.
  • Time in the tab title: while the timer runs, the remaining time and phase show in your browser tab, so you can see them from another tab.
  • Skip and reset: Skip jumps to the next phase; Reset returns to the first focus session.

Change any length or the number of sessions per round in the boxes under the timer. Your settings are saved in your browser for next time.

How to Run a Pomodoro Session

  1. Pick one task. Decide what you will work on before you press Start.
  2. Start the timer and work until the chime. If something unrelated comes up, jot it down and come back to it later.
  3. Take the break away from your screen. Stand up, stretch, get water.
  4. Repeat. After the fourth focus session, take the longer break.
  5. Reset when you finish for the day. The next round then starts from session one.

Changing the Lengths

25 and 5 minutes are the classic settings, but they are a starting point rather than a rule. Many people try 50 minutes of focus with a 10-minute break for work that needs a long run-up, or 15 and 3 for days when concentration is hard to find. Adjust the numbers until the rhythm feels sustainable. For a deeper walkthrough, read the Pomodoro technique guide.

Tips

Keep the tab open while you work. The remaining time is calculated from your device's clock, so it stays correct, but a browser that puts a background tab to sleep may delay the chime. Starting the timer also allows the page to play sound, so press Start before you rely on the alert. If you want a custom pattern with differently named steps, build it with the interval timer; for a single fixed block, use the countdown timer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is one Pomodoro?

Traditionally one Pomodoro is a 25-minute focus session followed by a 5-minute break. This timer uses those lengths by default and lets you change them.

How many Pomodoros before a long break?

By default the timer gives you a 15-minute long break after every fourth focus session. You can change the number of sessions per round and the break length.

Can I change the focus and break lengths?

Yes. Edit the focus, short break, long break and sessions-per-round boxes under the timer. Your changes are saved in your browser.

Does the timer move to the next phase automatically?

Yes. When a focus session or break ends, a chime plays and the next phase starts immediately. Use Skip if you want to move on early.

Will it keep running if I switch tabs?

Yes. The remaining time is based on your device's clock, so it stays accurate. The browser may delay the chime if it puts a background tab to sleep, and the tab title shows the time left.

Is the Pomodoro timer free?

Yes. It is free, needs no sign-up or download, and works on phones, tablets and computers.
