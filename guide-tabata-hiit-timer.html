How to Set Up a Tabata or HIIT Workout With an Interval Timer

What Tabata and HIIT are, the work-to-rest patterns people use, and a step-by-step guide to building each in the interval timer.

High-intensity interval training, usually shortened to HIIT, alternates short bursts of hard effort with periods of rest or easy movement. Tabata is one famous version of it. The hard part of doing either one well is not the exercise, it is the timing. If you have to look at a clock and count seconds, you are not putting everything into the effort. An interval timer takes that job away so you can concentrate on the movement.

What Tabata and HIIT actually mean

Tabata is a specific protocol: 20 seconds of all-out effort followed by 10 seconds of rest, repeated for 8 rounds, which comes to exactly four minutes. It is named after Dr. Izumi Tabata, a Japanese researcher who studied this pattern with athletes in the 1990s. The effort is meant to be genuinely maximal, which is why it is short.

HIIT is the broader family. Any session that alternates hard intervals with recovery counts. Common patterns include 30 seconds on and 30 off, 40 on and 20 off, or 45 on and 15 off, usually run for 6 to 12 rounds and often cycling through several different exercises.

Build a Tabata routine step by step

StopwatchPro's interval timer runs a list of named steps, each with a duration in seconds. It runs the list once from top to bottom and plays a sound at each change, so a Tabata block is a list of 16 steps. Here is how to set one up:

  1. Open the interval timer and clear the default steps so you start with a single step.
  2. Name the first step "Work" and set it to 20 seconds.
  3. Add a second step, name it "Rest" and set it to 10 seconds.
  4. Keep adding Work and Rest steps until you have eight of each, 16 steps in total.
  5. Type a name such as "Tabata 4 min" and press Save. The routine is stored in your browser, so next time you can load it with one tap.
  6. Press Start and keep the tab open. Each beep tells you the phase has changed.

Tip: you only need to type the pattern out once. Save it, and later sessions are a single tap.

Build a longer HIIT session

A single four-minute Tabata block is a good finisher, but a full session usually strings several together or uses a different exercise at each work step. A simple structure looks like this:

  • Warm-up (3 to 5 minutes): one step named "Warm-up" at 180 to 300 seconds of easy movement.
  • Main set: Work 40 seconds, Rest 20 seconds, repeated for the number of exercises you want. Name each work step after the exercise, for example "Squat jumps" or "Mountain climbers", so the screen tells you what is next.
  • Cool-down (3 to 5 minutes): one step named "Cool-down" at 180 to 300 seconds of walking and stretching.

Naming steps after exercises is one of the most useful things you can do. When you are breathing hard you do not want to remember a list.

Choosing a work-to-rest ratio

PatternRatioWho it suits
20 work / 10 rest2:1Experienced exercisers, short all-out efforts
40 work / 20 rest2:1Most intermediate trainees, mixed exercises
30 work / 30 rest1:1A solid starting point for most people
30 work / 60 rest1:2Beginners, heavy or technical movements

If your form breaks down in the last rounds, that is a sign to lengthen the rest or shorten the work, not to push through. The aim is to repeat good-quality efforts, not to finish exhausted.

Progressing over time

  1. Start with a ratio you can complete with good form, such as 30 seconds on and 30 off.
  2. When that feels manageable across a few sessions, change one thing at a time: add a round, lengthen the work or shorten the rest.
  3. Keep a note of what you did. Because the routine is saved, you only need to edit one number to make next week's session slightly harder.
  4. Take easy or rest days between hard interval days. Recovery is part of the training.

Safety notes

High-intensity exercise is demanding. Warm up first, choose movements you can do with good technique, and stop if you feel dizzy, faint or unwell. If you are new to exercise, are returning after a break, are pregnant, or have a heart, joint or other medical condition, talk to a doctor or qualified trainer before starting. Frequently Asked Questions

How long is a Tabata workout?

A single Tabata block is exactly four minutes: 8 rounds of 20 seconds of work and 10 seconds of rest. Many people do one or several blocks with a warm-up and cool-down.

How do I repeat rounds in StopwatchPro's interval timer?

The timer runs through your list of steps once. To repeat a pattern, add the work and rest steps again for each round, then save the routine so you can reload it later.

Is Tabata the same as HIIT?

Tabata is one specific form of HIIT, using 20 seconds of maximal work and 10 seconds of rest for 8 rounds. HIIT is the wider category of hard-and-easy interval training.

Will I hear when the phase changes?

Yes. A sound plays at each transition between steps. Keep the tab open and your device volume up.
