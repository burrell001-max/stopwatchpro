How Long to Steep Tea: Times and Temperatures for Every Type

Recommended steep times and water temperatures by tea type, and how to time them accurately.

Tea is forgiving, but time matters more than most people expect. Steep too short and the cup tastes weak and thin. Steep too long and it turns bitter or astringent. A countdown timer removes the guesswork so you get the same good cup each time.

Steeping times and temperatures at a glance

The figures below are common starting points for loose leaf or bagged tea, using about one teaspoon of leaf or one bag per cup. Check the packaging for your particular tea, and adjust to your own taste.

How to time your tea

  1. Heat the water to the right temperature. For delicate teas like green and white, let boiled water rest for a minute or two so it is not too hot.
  2. Pour the water over the tea and start the timer straight away.
  3. Open the countdown timer, enter the steep time, press Set and then Start.
  4. When the alert sounds, remove the tea bag or strain out the leaves. Do not leave them in.

If you often make the same tea, save the timer under a name such as "Green tea 2:30". Saved timers are kept in your browser, so they are ready next time.

Why temperature and time matter

Hot water extracts flavour compounds from the leaf. Tannins, which taste bitter and dry, are extracted more as the water gets hotter and the leaf sits longer. Green and white teas are more delicate, so cooler water and shorter times protect their flavour. Black tea and herbal teas stand up to boiling water and longer steeping.

Tips for a better cup

  • Taste at the early end of the range. You can always steep longer next time, but you cannot undo a bitter cup.
  • Use good water. Fresh, filtered water gives a cleaner flavour than water that has been boiled several times.
  • Do not squeeze the tea bag. This can release extra tannins and bitterness.
  • Try a second steep. Many loose leaf green, white and oolong teas give a good second or third cup, with a slightly longer time each round.
  • Warm the cup or pot first. It keeps the water from cooling too fast.

Other things to time with the same timer

The same countdown timer works for coffee brewing, such as a 4-minute French press, as well as eggs, pasta, baking and any kitchen task. For a recipe with several steps, such as simmer for 10 minutes and rest for 3, chain them in the interval timer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I steep black tea?

Usually 3 to 5 minutes in water just off the boil. Start at 3 minutes and go longer if you like it stronger.

Why is my green tea bitter?

The water may be too hot or the leaves left in too long. Try water of about 75 to 85 °C and a steep of 2 to 3 minutes.

Can I steep tea for too long?

Yes. Over-steeping extracts more tannins, which makes tea bitter and astringent. Remove the leaves or bag when the timer ends.

Can I save my favourite steeping times?

Yes. Name a countdown, such as "Green tea 2:30", and save it. Saved countdowns are stored in your browser.
