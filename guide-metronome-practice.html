How to Practice With a Metronome: Tempo, BPM and Building Speed

What BPM means, how to choose a tempo, and a routine for building speed without losing accuracy.

A metronome gives you a steady beat to play against. That sounds simple, but it is one of the most effective tools in musical practice, because it shows you exactly where your timing wanders. Used well, it improves accuracy first and speed second. Here is how to get the most out of it.

What BPM means

BPM stands for beats per minute. At 60 BPM, the metronome clicks once every second. At 120 BPM, it clicks twice a second. A higher BPM means a faster tempo. StopwatchPro's metronome covers 30 to 240 BPM with a slider, which is enough for nearly all written music.

The same BPM can feel different depending on how a piece divides the beat. A passage of quarter notes at 60 BPM has one note per click. A passage of sixteenth notes at 60 BPM has four notes per click. So the tempo that is right for you depends on the piece, not only the number.

Traditional tempo markings

MarkingMeaningApproximate BPM
LargoVery slow40 to 60
AdagioSlow66 to 76
AndanteWalking pace76 to 108
ModeratoModerate108 to 120
AllegroFast, lively120 to 156
PrestoVery fast168 and above

These ranges are rough. Composers, editions and performers differ, and a marking in a score is a hint about character as much as a number.

Find your starting tempo

  1. Choose a short passage, such as a scale, a difficult bar or a four-bar phrase.
  2. Set a tempo that feels almost too slow. At this speed you should be able to play the passage correctly and relaxed.
  3. Play it three times in a row. If all three are clean, that is your starting tempo.

Build speed step by step

  1. Raise the tempo by a small amount, about 4 to 5 BPM.
  2. Play the passage three times. If it is still clean, go up again.
  3. If mistakes appear, drop back one step and stay there until it feels easy.
  4. Stop while the practice is still accurate. Rushing in the last few minutes teaches mistakes.

This approach builds speed on top of accuracy. Pushing the tempo before a passage is secure trains tension and errors, and those are hard to unlearn.

Use tap tempo to find a song's BPM

If you want to practice along with a recording and you do not know its BPM, use the tap-tempo button. Tap in time with the music for several beats, and the metronome works out the tempo from the gap between your taps. Then use that tempo to practice slowly and bring the piece up to speed.

Beyond a steady click

  • Follow the visual beat. The pulsing circle on screen is a second cue that is helpful in noisy rooms and when the click is hard to hear over your instrument.
  • Play off the beat. Try placing notes between clicks to check your sense of subdivision.
  • Stop and start. Practise a bar with the click, stop the click, keep playing, then restart it to see whether you stayed in time. This builds an internal pulse.
  • Change the emphasis. Think of the click as beats 2 and 4 rather than 1 and 3, which is how many styles of music feel the beat.

Common mistakes

  • Starting too fast and never going back to a comfortable tempo.
  • Practising only at one speed. Alternate slow, medium and performance tempos.
  • Staring at the metronome instead of listening. The goal is to hear when you are early or late.
  • Forgetting to rest. Short, focused sessions beat long, tired ones. A countdown timer can keep practice blocks short and consistent.

Beyond music

A metronome is also useful for matching running cadence, rowing or cycling rhythm, speech pacing and paced breathing. Set a tempo that suits the activity and match your movement to it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What BPM should I practice at?

Start with a tempo at which you can play the passage cleanly three times in a row, even if it feels too slow. Then raise it by 4 to 5 BPM at a time.

What range of BPM does the metronome cover?

The slider covers 30 to 240 BPM, which suits nearly all written music.

How do I find the BPM of a song?

Use the tap-tempo button. Tap in time with the music for several beats and the metronome sets the tempo from your taps.

Is the online metronome accurate enough for practice?

It runs in your browser, so timing can vary slightly with your device's performance. It is well suited to practice. For recording or critical timing, check it against a trusted reference.
