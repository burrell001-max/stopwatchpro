How to Use StopwatchPro

A quick manual for each of the nine timing tools, plus tips that apply everywhere.

⚙️ Before you start

  • Nothing to install. Every tool runs in your browser on a phone, tablet or computer. There is no account or sign-up.
  • Navigation. Use the tool menu at the top of each page to switch between tools.
  • Keep the tab open and visible for anything important. Browsers and phones can slow background tabs to save battery, which can make timers drift or alerts arrive late.
  • Full screen. Most tools have a full-screen button for large digits you can read from across a room. On a computer you can also press F.
  • Appearance. Use the moon button for dark mode and the palette button for colours, large text and high contrast. Your choices are remembered in your browser.
  • Language. The selector at the top changes the interface to Spanish, French, German or Dutch. The long written guides are in English.
  • Saved items (countdowns, interval routines and alarms) are stored in your browser only. They do not sync between devices, and clearing browser data removes them.

⏱ Online Stopwatch

  • Start / Stop: click the main button or press Space.
  • Lap: while it is running, click Lap or press L to record a lap. The lap list shows both lap time and split time, with your fastest and slowest laps highlighted.
  • Reset: stop the clock and click Reset, or press R, to return to zero. Laps are cleared as well.
  • More: see the stopwatch page for how laps and splits differ and tips for accurate timing.

🏁 Lap & Split Timer

  • The lap and split timer works like the stopwatch but is set up for repeated segments: laps around a track, legs of a race, sections of an exam.
  • Press Lap at the end of each segment. The main clock keeps running.
  • Read your lap list after you stop. Laps are kept only for the current session, so write down results you want to keep. Our guide to pacing a 5K shows a full example.

⏳ Countdown Timer

  • Set a duration: enter the length of time, press Set, then press Start. The timer counts down and sounds an alert at zero.
  • Save a timer: give a countdown a name and press Save to keep it. Saved timers appear in a list, ready to load next time.
  • Ideas: cooking and tea, study sessions, presentations and rest between sets. See the countdown timer page for more.

🔄 Interval Timer

  • Build a routine: add steps, give each a name and a length in seconds, for example "Work 40" and "Rest 20".
  • Repeat a pattern: the routine runs from the first step to the last, so add the steps again for each round you want.
  • Sound cues: a sound plays at every change between steps. You can pause and resume with the Start/Stop button.
  • Save routines under a name to reuse them. For a worked example, read our Tabata and HIIT guide.

⏰ Alarm Clock

  • Choose a time, add an optional label and pick a sound (beep, chime, digital or bell). Use Preview to hear it first.
  • Press Add alarm. The display shows how long until your next alarm.
  • Saved alarms can be tested or deleted from the list. The tab must stay open for an alarm to sound, so do not rely on it for anything critical.

🕒 Digital Clock

  • Shows the current time (24-hour format, with seconds) and the date, taken from your device.
  • Use full screen for a large wall clock, for example on a projector or spare monitor.

♟ Chess Clock

  • Set the time: enter the minutes per side. Both players get the same time.
  • Play: press Start game. Tap your side after each move to stop your clock and start your opponent's.
  • Low time: a side is highlighted when it has 30 seconds or less. The game stops when either clock reaches zero.
  • There is no increment. Read our guide to chess time controls for what that means.

🎵 Metronome

  • Tempo: drag the slider anywhere from 30 to 240 beats per minute.
  • Tap tempo: tap the button in time with a song and the metronome sets the BPM for you.
  • Visual cue: a pulsing circle marks each beat, in addition to the click. See how to practise with a metronome.

🔢 Tally Counter

  • Tap + to add one and − to take one away. The count never goes below zero.
  • On a computer, use + and - to count and R to reset.
  • The count is held for your session only. Refreshing the page resets it.
