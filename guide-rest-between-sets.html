How Long to Rest Between Sets

Typical rest periods for strength, muscle growth and endurance work, and a simple way to time them.

Rest between sets is easy to get wrong in both directions. Scroll your phone for five minutes and your session drags on and loses focus. Cut the rest too short and your next set suffers. Timing your rest makes your training consistent and your workouts more efficient.

Common rest ranges by goal

These are typical guidelines used in strength and conditioning. They are starting points, and individual needs vary.

Training goalTypical rest between setsWhy
Maximal strength (heavy, low reps)2 to 5 minutesYour muscles and nervous system need time to recover for heavy lifts
Muscle growth (moderate weight, 6 to 12 reps)60 to 120 seconds, longer for big liftsBalances recovery with total workload
Muscular endurance (light weight, high reps)30 to 60 secondsShort rests keep the demand on endurance high
Circuit training15 to 30 seconds between stationsKeeps the heart rate elevated

Compound movements like squats and deadlifts usually need more rest than small isolation exercises like curls.

How to time your rest

  1. Open the countdown timer and set your rest time, for example 90 seconds.
  2. Save it with a name such as "Rest 90" so you can load it in a single tap.
  3. Finish your set and start the timer. Rest until the alert sounds.
  4. Start your next set as soon as the timer ends.

Create a few saved timers for the rest times you use most, such as 60, 90 and 180 seconds.

For timed efforts: use the interval timer

If your workout is built around fixed work and rest periods, such as 40 seconds of work and 20 of rest, the interval timer runs the whole sequence for you. See our guide to setting up Tabata and HIIT for the step-by-step setup.

Signs you need more or less rest

  • Need more: your reps fall sharply from set to set, your form breaks down, or you feel dizzy or very out of breath at the start of the next set.
  • Can use less: you feel fully recovered well before the timer ends, and your performance does not drop between sets.

Tips

  • Track your rest as carefully as your weights and reps. If you want to compare one week with the next, keep the rest time the same.
  • Use the rest period to hydrate, note your reps or prepare the next exercise, not to scroll through social media.
  • For heavy lifts, do not rush. Cutting rest short to finish sooner is a common reason for poor sets.
  • Warm-up sets need much less rest than your working sets.

This is general information, not medical advice. If you are new to training or have a health condition, speak to a doctor or qualified coach before you start.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I rest between sets to build muscle?

A common range is 60 to 120 seconds for moderate-rep sets, and longer for heavy compound lifts.

How long should I rest for strength training?

Heavy, low-rep sets are typically rested for 2 to 5 minutes so you can recover for the next lift.

Can I save common rest times?

Yes. Set a countdown, give it a name such as "Rest 90", and save it. Saved timers stay in your browser.

Should I use a stopwatch or a countdown for rest?

A countdown is better, because it alerts you when the rest ends. A stopwatch is useful if you want to measure how long you actually rest.
